Why Voice Recordings Get Rejected: 7 Mistakes to Avoid

Last updated: November 11, 2026

In voice data projects, a recording can be rejected for reasons that have nothing to do with how well you speak. Most of them are technical and easy to prevent once you know them.

1. Sound problems

Background noise, room echo, a running fan or air conditioner, and a phone held too far away all make the voice harder to use. Read how to reduce echo and noise.

2. Level problems

A recording that is too loud is clipped and sounds distorted. One that is too quiet is hard to use. Volume that jumps between files is also a problem. See what audio clipping is.

3. Speaking problems

  • Skipping or changing words from the script
  • Reading too fast
  • Coughs, breaths and mouth sounds left in the take

4. File problems

  • Wrong format or sample rate
  • Wrong amount of silence at the start or end. Follow the project guide; see silence at the start and end.
  • Wrong file names. See file naming conventions.
  • Duplicate files

5. Not reading the instructions

Many rejections come from small details in the project instructions. Read them fully before you record, and keep them open while you work.

6. Test before you submit

  • Record a short sample first.
  • Listen with headphones.
  • Check the whole batch before delivery.
