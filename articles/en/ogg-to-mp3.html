How to Convert OGG to MP3 So It Plays on Every Device

Last updated: 28 October 2026

OGG is an open container, and inside it there is usually Vorbis audio (sometimes Opus). It offers good quality at small sizes, but some devices, apps and editing programs do not accept it. MP3 plays almost everywhere, which is why people convert OGG to MP3 when they need a file that is sure to play.

When is this conversion useful?

  • A player, phone or car that cannot play OGG.
  • An editing or upload tool that rejects OGG.
  • You want to send a file to someone and do not know their device.

For a comparison of the two formats in quality and compatibility, read OGG vs MP3.

Which bitrate should you choose?

The original file is already lossy, and converting it to MP3 adds a second round of lossy compression. To limit the damage:

  • Choose a bitrate equal to the original file's, or slightly higher. On Windows you can often find the file's bitrate under "Properties", then "Details".
  • Going much higher does not improve quality; it only makes the file bigger.
  • For speech, 96 to 128 kbps is often enough. For music, 192 and above.

If you still have the uncompressed original (WAV or FLAC), convert from that straight to MP3 instead of going through OGG. For more on bitrates read CBR vs VBR.

If an OGG file will not open

Decoding depends on the browser. Try Chrome or Firefox; some browsers do not support every kind of OGG (such as Opus). If the problem remains, check that the file is not damaged and that it opens in another player.

Frequently asked questions

Does converting OGG to MP3 reduce quality?

Slightly, since both formats are lossy. The difference is usually small at a suitable bitrate, but do not keep converting between compressed formats.
