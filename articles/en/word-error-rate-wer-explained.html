What Is Word Error Rate (WER) and How Do You Calculate It?

Last updated: November 17, 2026

Word error rate, or WER, is the standard way to measure how accurate a transcript is. It is used to check speech recognition and also to review human transcription. The lower the number, the closer the transcript is to the correct text.

The formula

WER = (replaced + removed + added) ÷ number of words in the original

  • Replaced: a word was swapped for a different one.
  • Removed: a word is in the original but missing from the transcript.
  • Added: a word is in the transcript but not in the original.

A worked example

The original has 10 words. In the transcript, one word is replaced, one is missing and two are added. The errors are 1 + 1 + 2 = 4. So WER = 4 ÷ 10 = 40%.

Things to know

  • WER can be above 100%, if the transcript adds many extra words.
  • The original text is the reference. Always put the correct text there.
  • Decide before you start what counts as an error. Punctuation, capital letters and Arabic diacritics are often ignored, so that only real word differences are counted.
  • For Arabic, decide whether letter variants such as أ and ا count as errors. It changes the number.
  • WER treats every word the same. A wrong small word counts as much as a wrong name.
