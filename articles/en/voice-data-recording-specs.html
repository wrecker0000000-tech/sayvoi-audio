Preparing Voice Recordings for Data Projects: Typical Specs and a Checklist

Last updated: October 24, 2026

Companies that collect speech data for voice assistants, speech recognition or wake-word training are strict about how recordings are delivered. A recording that sounds fine to a person may still be rejected because of its format, level or length. This guide covers the requirements you meet most often.

Important: every project has its own specification. Treat the values below as typical examples, and always follow the document you receive from the client.

Typical technical requirements

  • Format: WAV (uncompressed PCM), usually 16-bit.
  • Sample rate: 16 kHz is very common for speech; some projects ask for 24, 44.1 or 48 kHz.
  • Channels: mono in most speech projects.
  • Duration: one sentence or phrase per file, sometimes with a maximum length.
  • Silence: a short, clean silence at the beginning and end of each file, often about half a second to one second.

Recording quality

  • A quiet room without echo. Soft furniture and curtains help a lot.
  • A steady distance from the microphone for the whole session.
  • No clipping. Peaks should stay clearly below 0 dBFS (digital full scale). Many projects ask for peaks somewhere between -6 and -1 dBFS, so check your specification.
  • No heavy processing. Avoid aggressive noise reduction, compression or effects unless the project allows it.
  • A consistent level across files from the same speaker.

Record high, convert once

If your device records at 44.1 or 48 kHz and the project needs 16 kHz mono, convert each file once from the original. Do not convert files several times, and keep the originals until the client accepts the delivery. File size to expect

A 16 kHz, 16-bit, mono WAV takes about 32 KB per second, roughly 1.9 MB per minute. A 3-second sentence is therefore about 96 KB.

Delivery checklist

  1. Format, sample rate and channels match the specification.
  2. Every file is within the allowed duration.
  3. Silence at the start and end is present and consistent.
  4. No clipping, no clicks, no background noise.
  5. No duplicated or missing sentences.
  6. File names follow the required naming pattern, without spaces or unexpected characters.
  7. Files are grouped by speaker, in the structure the client asked for.

Checking these points before delivery saves rounds of corrections and rejected files.
