How to Clean Audio Recordings: Reduce Noise, Remove Silence, Fix Volume

Last updated: November 12, 2026

Most voice recordings need the same three fixes before they are ready to deliver: less background noise, no extra silence, and a steady volume. The order you do them in matters. This guide explains the right order and what each step can and cannot do.

The right order

  1. Reduce steady noise first. Noise reduction learns what the noise sounds like, so it works best on the original recording.
  2. Remove silence second. Once the noise is lower, quiet parts are easy to detect. With a noisy file, the tool may think the noise is sound and cut nothing.
  3. Normalize the volume last. Every earlier step changes the level, so the final volume should be set at the end.

Step 1: Reduce steady noise

This works well on noise that stays the same all the time: a fan, an air conditioner, hiss, or room hum. It does not work on voices in the background, music, or sudden sounds like a door. Two practical tips:

  • Leave one or two short pauses in the recording, because the noise is learned from the quietest parts.
  • Start with a light setting. Strong settings remove more noise but can make speech sound slightly watery. For the cause of noise and echo, read how to reduce echo and noise in recordings.

Step 2: Remove silence

You can trim only the start and end, or also shorten long pauses in the middle. A short margin should stay at the edges so the first and last words are not cut. Many projects ask for a specific amount of silence, so check the guide first. See silence at the start and end of a recording.

Step 3: Normalize the volume

Normalizing sets the final level, so files from different sessions sound alike. You can set the loudest point, or the average loudness. The difference is explained in peak or loudness: how to normalize volume. Normalizing does not repair a recording that was already distorted; for that, read audio clipping and distortion.

Cleaning many files at once

If you have hundreds of recordings, set the options once and apply them to every file. The Sayvoi Audio Cleaner accepts many files or a ZIP, and gives you one ZIP back. Before you run the full batch:

  • Preview the first file and listen to it before and after.
  • Check one file from a different speaker or device, because settings that suit one microphone may not suit another.
  • Keep your originals. The tool never changes them and saves new files ending with _clean.

WAV or MP3?

For data projects, keep WAV. MP3 is smaller but it is compressed. See the difference between WAV and MP3.
