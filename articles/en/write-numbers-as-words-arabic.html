How to Write Numbers as Arabic Words: Egyptian and Standard Arabic

Last updated: November 6, 2026

Speech data and transcripts often need numbers written the way people say them, not as digits. In Arabic this is harder than in English, because the words change with the dialect and with the number itself. The Sayvoi numbers-to-words tool does the conversion for you.

Why digits are a problem

The number 2026 can be read in several ways. A reader of Egyptian Arabic and a news presenter will not say it identically, and a speech model trained on text with digits does not know which one you meant.

Egyptian or Standard Arabic

  • Egyptian: how people actually speak, for example 15 is read as khamastāshar.
  • Standard (Fusha): the formal form, for example khamsata ʿashar.

Choose the one that matches your speakers and your project guidelines.

What it can convert

  • Plain numbers and large numbers
  • Dates and times
  • Currencies and percentages
  • Phone numbers, read digit by digit

Check the result

Always read the output once. Context decides some choices, such as whether a number is a quantity, a year or a phone number, and a short review prevents embarrassing mistakes. See also how to handle numbers in transcription guidelines.
