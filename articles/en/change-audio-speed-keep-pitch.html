How to Slow Down or Speed Up Audio Without Changing the Pitch

Last updated: November 15, 2026

Sometimes a recording is too fast to transcribe, or too slow for a script. You can change its speed in two different ways, and they sound very different. This guide explains both and when to use each.

Two ways to change speed

  • Keep the pitch (time-stretching). The audio becomes slower or faster, but the voice stays at the same pitch. This is the natural choice for speech.
  • Change the pitch with the speed (resampling). This is like playing a tape faster or slower. The voice becomes higher when faster and deeper when slower. There is no processing artifact, but the voice sounds different.

What speeds work well

For speech, time-stretching sounds natural between about 80 and 125 percent. Beyond that, the voice can start to sound slightly watery or robotic. If you need a very different speed, listen carefully before using the result.

Do it in your browser

  1. Open the Sayvoi Audio Speed Changer and drop your file, many files, or a ZIP.
  2. Type the speed in percent. 100 is the original speed, 80 is slower, 120 is faster. You can enter several speeds, such as 80, 120, to get one file for each.
  3. Choose to keep the pitch or change it with the speed.
  4. Preview the first file before and after, then download WAV or MP3.

Each result is a new file whose name ends with the speed, for example name_80pct.wav. Your originals are never changed.

An important warning for data projects

Stretched audio is not the same as a real recording at a different speed. If a project asks for slow or fast speech, speakers usually must really speak that way. Read the project specification before you submit processed audio. See also voice data recording specifications.
