How to Convert FLAC to MP3 for Free While Keeping Good Quality

Last updated: 28 October 2026

FLAC is a lossless format: it keeps the sound identical to the source, but the files are relatively large and not every device or app can play them. That is why many people convert FLAC to MP3 to play on a phone or in the car, or to send files more easily.

When should you convert FLAC to MP3?

  • Your device, car or player does not read FLAC.
  • Your phone storage is limited.
  • You want to send or upload files where size is capped.
  • The app or site you use does not accept FLAC.

How much smaller does it get?

One minute of CD-quality audio (stereo, 44.1 kHz, 16-bit):

FormatApproximate size per minute
WAV (uncompressed)10.6 MB
FLACOften between half and two thirds of that, depending on the music
MP3 at 320 kbps2.4 MB
MP3 at 192 kbps1.44 MB
MP3 at 128 kbps0.96 MB

Example: a 48-minute album takes only about 69 MB as MP3 at 192 kbps.

Important tips

  • Keep your original FLAC files. Converting to MP3 removes detail that cannot come back, and you can always make a new MP3 from the original.
  • Do not convert MP3 to FLAC to improve quality. The file only gets bigger. Read lossless vs lossy audio.
  • Check the file details afterwards: the track name and album art may not carry over automatically, so verify them, and add them with a tag editor if they are missing.
  • For more on picking a bitrate, read CBR vs VBR.

Frequently asked questions

Does quality suffer?

Yes, because MP3 is a lossy format. But at 192 kbps and above, many listeners find it hard to tell the difference in everyday listening. Try it by ear on a track you know before converting the whole collection.

Are my files uploaded to a server?

No. Conversion happens inside your browser, so your files stay on your device.

How many files can I convert for free?

Up to 15 files a day with no account. Files under 100 MB each are recommended.
