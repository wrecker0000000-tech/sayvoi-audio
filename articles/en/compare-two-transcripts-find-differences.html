How to Compare Two Transcripts and Find the Differences

Last updated: November 17, 2026

When two people transcribe the same recording, or when a text is edited, you need to know exactly what changed. Reading both versions side by side is slow and easy to get wrong. A text comparison shows the differences in seconds.

What a comparison shows

  • Removed: words that are in the original but not in the changed text.
  • Added: words that are in the changed text but not in the original.
  • Replaced: a word that was swapped for another one.
  • Same: everything that did not change.

Step by step

  1. Paste the original text on one side. This is your reference.
  2. Paste the changed text on the other side. You can also load a text file, such as TXT, SRT or VTT.
  3. Choose to compare by words, lines or characters. Words is best for transcripts.
  4. Choose the options that match your work, then press Compare.
  5. Read the result. Red crossed-out words were removed, and green words were added.

Which options to choose

  • Ignore punctuation: so that a comma or a full stop is not counted as a difference.
  • Ignore upper and lower case: useful for English transcripts.
  • Ignore Arabic diacritics: so that a word with and without tashkeel is the same word.
  • Treat similar Arabic letters as the same: أ إ آ as ا, ى as ي, and ة as ه. Use it when the spelling of these letters is not what you are checking.
  • Ignore extra spaces and empty lines: so that spacing does not create false differences.

Good to know

The comparison finds the longest common sequence of words, so a single added word does not mark the whole sentence as different. Use lines for very long texts, or split them into smaller parts. The tool runs on your device and your texts are not uploaded.

Where it helps

  • Checking a subcontractor's transcript against a reviewed one.
  • Seeing what an editor changed in a script.
  • Measuring how far a machine transcript is from the correct text. Read what the word error rate means.
