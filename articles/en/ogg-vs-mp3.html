OGG vs MP3: Which Format Should You Choose?

Last updated: October 24, 2026

MP3 is the format everyone knows. OGG is an open alternative that is less visible but just as capable. Which one is better depends on where the file will be used.

The basics

  • MP3: introduced in the 1990s and supported by practically every player, phone, car stereo and website.
  • OGG: an open container, most often holding audio compressed with Vorbis. It is free of licensing concerns and widely used in games, open-source software and some web audio.

Quality at the same size

Both are lossy. At the same bitrate, OGG generally sounds a little better than MP3, and the gap is clearest at low bitrates, such as 64 to 96 kbps. At high bitrates, such as 192 kbps and above, most listeners cannot tell them apart.

Compatibility

This is where MP3 wins. Almost everything plays MP3. OGG support is good in modern browsers and on Android, but some players, older devices, editing tools and upload forms do not accept it. If you do not know where the file will end up, MP3 is the safe choice.

Which to pick

SituationChoice
Sharing with many people or unknown devicesMP3
A form or platform that says "MP3"MP3
Small files at low bitrate for a website or gameOGG
You prefer open formatsOGG
Master copies, archives, datasetsNeither: use WAV or FLAC
