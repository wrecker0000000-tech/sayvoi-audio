MP4 vs MKV vs WebM vs MOV: Video Formats Explained

Last updated: October 24, 2026

People often say "video format" when they mean two different things: the container (the file type, such as MP4) and the codec (how the picture and sound are compressed inside it). Understanding the difference makes every conversion decision easier.

Container vs codec

A container is like a box that holds a video track, an audio track, subtitles and metadata. The codec is how each track inside the box was compressed. A file called .mp4 usually holds H.264 video and AAC audio, but the same container can hold other codecs.

The common containers

ContainerUsually holdsStrengthsWeaknesses
MP4H.264 or H.265 video, AAC audioPlays almost everywhere: phones, browsers, TVs, social platformsLess flexible with many tracks or subtitle types
MOVSimilar to MP4, from AppleCommon from iPhones and Apple softwareFewer players outside Apple's world
MKVAlmost any codec, many audio and subtitle tracksVery flexible, popular for movies and archivesLimited support in browsers and on some devices
WebMVP8, VP9 or AV1 video, Vorbis or Opus audioOpen and designed for the webLess support on some Apple devices and editing software

Changing the container vs changing the codec

  • Changing only the container (called remuxing) copies the tracks into a new box. It is fast and loses no quality, but works only if the new container supports the codecs inside.
  • Changing the codec means decoding and encoding again. It takes much longer, and every lossy re-encode loses some quality.

Which should you choose?

  • Sharing with anyone: MP4 with H.264 and AAC.
  • Video on your own website: MP4 or WebM.
  • Keeping many tracks or subtitles: MKV.
  • Editing on a Mac or iPhone: MOV or MP4.
