Why Recordings Need Silence at the Start and End (and How Much)

Last updated: October 24, 2026

Many voice-data projects require a short stretch of silence before the first word and after the last. It looks like a small detail, but it protects the recording in several ways.

Why it matters

  • It protects the words. Without margin, the first or last sound can be cut off by a trimming tool or by the recording app starting late.
  • It helps processing. Speech recognition and detection tools use the quiet parts to learn what the background sounds like.
  • It makes files consistent. A fixed margin makes a batch easier to cut, align and annotate.

How much is typical?

Specifications vary, so read yours. A common range is 0.3 to 0.5 seconds at the start and the same at the end. Too little risks clipped words. Too much wastes storage and annotator time, and some projects set a maximum as well.

"Silence" is not digital zero

A real recording is never perfectly silent: the margin contains the quiet sound of the room, called room tone. That is what you want. A stretch of exact digital zero is unnatural, can make a tool think the file has been edited, and some checkers flag it. If you must add silence in an editor, paste in a short sample of the room tone instead of generating blank audio.

How to record it correctly

  1. Press record, then wait about half a second before you speak.
  2. Finish the sentence, then stay quiet for half a second before pressing stop.
  3. Do not touch the phone during the margin, because the tap of your finger will be recorded.
