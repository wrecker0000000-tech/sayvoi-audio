Why You Should Not Upload Private Recordings to Online Converters

Last updated: October 24, 2026

Many online audio converters work by sending your file to a server, converting it there, and sending the result back. That is convenient, but it also means a copy of your recording passes through, and may stay on, a computer you do not control. For private interviews, client recordings or anything covered by a confidentiality agreement, that matters.

What can go wrong with upload-based converters

  • A copy leaves your device. You usually cannot see where it is stored or who can access it.
  • Unclear retention. Some services delete files after a while, others do not say.
  • Other people's voices. A recording often contains voices of people who never agreed to have their audio sent to a third party.
  • Contract and policy risks. Many projects forbid sharing recordings with outside services.
  • Slower for large files, because everything must be uploaded and downloaded again.

How in-browser conversion works

Modern browsers can decode, process and encode audio on their own. When you add a file to Sayvoi Audio Converter, the browser reads it from your device, converts it using code that runs on your computer or phone, and gives you the result to download. The tools used for the conversion are part of the website, but your audio is never sent to our servers.

How to verify it yourself

  1. On a computer, open the page and press F12 to open the browser's developer tools.
  2. Go to the Network tab.
  3. Convert a file.
  4. Look at the requests that appear. You will see the page's own files loading, but no upload of your audio.

What the site stores

To count your free daily conversions, the page keeps a small counter in your browser's local storage. It is not sent anywhere. Read the full details in our privacy policy.
