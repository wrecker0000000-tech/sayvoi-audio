Slow Audio Down for Transcription, Practice and Listening

Last updated: November 15, 2026

A slower recording can make a hard sentence much easier to catch. People use it for transcription, language practice, checking pronunciation and reviewing interviews. This guide gives practical speeds and a simple workflow.

Best speeds for each job

  • Transcription of fast speech: 80 to 90 percent keeps the voice natural and gives you time to type.
  • Language practice: 70 to 85 percent helps you hear individual sounds. Go back to normal speed as you improve.
  • Fast review of long interviews: 110 to 125 percent saves time and still sounds clear.

Keep the pitch for speech

Use the pitch-keeping mode so the voice does not become deeper or higher. See how to change speed without changing the pitch for the limits.

A simple workflow

  1. Drop the file into the Audio Speed Changer.
  2. Type one speed, or several such as 80 and 120.
  3. Preview the result, then download it. Each file gets the speed in its name.
  4. Keep the original beside the slower copy.

Make many files at once

Drop many files or a ZIP and you get one ZIP back, with a copy for each speed. Everything is processed on your device, so private recordings stay private.

Remember

Slowed audio is for listening, not for delivering as new data. For project recordings, read the specification first, and check your files with a quality check before delivery.
